Fiber: the nutrient almost nobody gets enough of

Most adults eat well below the recommended amount of fiber each day —and that has a real cost for your digestion, your heart, and your blood sugar.

By CalorIA Scan · August 4, 2026 · 2 min read

The short version

  • The recommended fiber intake is about 25 g a day for adult women and 38 g for adult men —but the average adult gets only around 15 g.
  • Fiber isn't one nutrient: soluble fiber helps lower cholesterol and stabilize blood sugar; insoluble fiber improves digestive transit.
  • You don't need supplements: beans, oats, lentils, apples with the skin on, and vegetables already get you most of the way to your daily goal.

How much fiber you should eat (and how much you actually eat)

Nutrition guidelines recommend around 25 grams of fiber a day for adult women and 38 grams for adult men, according to the U.S. Institute of Medicine. In practice, the average adult on a Western diet gets only about 15 grams a day —well below that target. It's one of the most consistent nutrition gaps out there, and unlike other nutrients, it barely gets talked about.

Why this gap actually matters

Fiber does more than "help you go." Soluble fiber —the kind that dissolves in water, found in oats, beans and apples— slows sugar absorption and helps lower LDL cholesterol. Insoluble fiber —found in whole grains and vegetables— adds bulk to stool and improves digestive transit. Together, adequate fiber intake is linked to lower cardiovascular risk and better blood sugar control, according to the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health.

It's not about a supplement, it's about your plate

You don't need powders or capsules to close the gap. Half a cup of cooked black beans has about 7-8 grams of fiber; an apple with the skin on, about 4 grams; half a cup of raw oats, another 4 grams. Swapping white rice for brown, leaving the skin on fruits and vegetables when you can, and adding one serving of legumes a day are small changes that add up fast.

How to know if your diet is already giving you enough

The simplest way to know is to add up what you eat on a typical day and compare it to the 25-38 gram target.
