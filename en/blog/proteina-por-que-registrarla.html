Protein: why it's worth tracking and how CalorIA Scan makes it easy

Almost everyone counts calories, but very few people know how much protein they actually eat. Here's why it's worth measuring and how to do it without the hassle.

By CalorIA Scan · October 19, 2026 · 2 min read

The short version

  • The general recommendation for a healthy adult is about 0.8 grams of protein per kilo of body weight a day, and that number is a minimum, not an ideal target for everyone.
  • It's easy to fall short without noticing: protein is often spread unevenly across meals, with breakfasts and dinners that have almost none.
  • CalorIA Scan estimates the protein on your plate from a photo, lets you adjust ingredients, and compares your day against the goal you set.

Why protein matters so much

Protein is what your body uses to build and repair muscle, skin, enzymes and immune defenses. It isn't stored the way fat is, so you need to eat it every day. Meals with good protein also tend to keep you full for longer, which helps with grazing between meals.

How much do you need?

The recommendation for a healthy adult is about 0.8 grams per kilo of body weight a day: around 56 grams if you weigh 70 kilos. The US Dietary Guidelines also express it as 10% to 35% of your daily calories.

Keep in mind: 0.8 g/kg is the amount to avoid deficiency, not necessarily the best amount for everyone. If you train, are recovering from an injury, or are an older adult, you may need more. And if you have kidney disease or another medical condition, check with your doctor or dietitian before increasing it.

Why it's so easy to fall short

If you have sweet bread and coffee for breakfast, a plate of rice with a little stew for lunch and cereal for dinner, you may hit your calories but not your protein. The Mexican diet has excellent sources —beans, lentils, eggs, chicken, fish, queso fresco— but we tend to pile them into one meal and leave the others with almost none.

An egg gives you about 6 grams and a cup of cooked beans about 15. Those amounts are hard to eyeball, which is exactly why they're worth measuring.

Tracking your protein without the hassle

Weighing every ingredient isn't sustainable for almost anyone. The practical approach is to measure a little, and measure well: see how much protein you've had so far today, spot which meal falls short, and adjust the next one. After a few days of logging, you start to recognize which dishes give you a lot and which give you almost none.

You don't need to obsess over grams. It's enough to know roughly how much protein you eat and spread it better across your meals. Start by logging one normal week and see where it falls short.
